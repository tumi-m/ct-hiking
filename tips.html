Tips & Safety

Everything you need to know before heading out on the trail

Never underestimate the mountain. Always carry:

  • Water — minimum 2 litres per person for a half-day hike
  • Sunscreen & sun hat — UV is extreme above 1,000m
  • Snacks — high-energy foods: nuts, dried fruit, bars
  • Layered clothing — temperatures drop rapidly on the mountain
  • Rain jacket — Table Mountain creates its own weather
  • Sturdy hiking boots — especially for rocky and wet trails
  • First aid kit — plasters, antiseptic, blister treatment
  • Charged phone — for navigation and emergency calls
  • Headlamp or torch — in case you're out after dark
  • Trail map or downloaded GPS — don't rely solely on cell signal
  • Always tell someone where you're going and when you expect to return
  • Never hike alone on remote trails — buddy system always
  • Turn around if the weather changes suddenly — Cape Town weather is famously unpredictable
  • Do not attempt summit routes in strong southeasterly wind (Cape Doctor)
  • Table Mountain & Lion's Head close when the weather is dangerous — check TMNP social media before going
  • Stick to marked trails — going off-trail can damage fynbos and you can get lost
  • Respect cutoff times on CapeNature reserves (usually 18:00)
  • Do not hike under the influence of alcohol or drugs
  • Emergency number: 107 (City of Cape Town), 112 (any network), WSAR: 021 948 9900

When to hike:

  • Cape Town is a winter-rainfall region. Summers (Nov–Mar) are dry, hot, and windy
  • Winter (Jun–Aug) brings rain and snow on higher peaks — trails can become dangerous
  • Best hiking: September–November (spring) and March–May (autumn)
  • Start hikes early to avoid afternoon heat and thunderstorms

Stream and river water:

  • Mountain stream water in the Western Cape is generally safe to drink but bring your own to be sure
  • Avoid drinking from streams below farming areas
  • Check river levels before kloofing or river trails — flash floods are a real risk after rain
  • Snakes — Puff adders and Cape cobras are common. Watch where you step and put your hands. Shake out boots before putting them on
  • Bees — Cape Nature warns of bee presence on trails. Move away calmly if you encounter a swarm; do not swat
  • Ticks — Apply DEET-based repellent before hiking. Check your skin and scalp after every hike
  • Baboons — Common on Table Mountain and Cape Peninsula. Never feed them. Do not make eye contact or run. Keep your bag closed
  • Cape Leopard — Extremely rare but present in Cederberg. They avoid humans
  • Seals & seabirds — Keep your distance at Robberg and coastal reserves — they're wild animals

SANParks (Table Mountain National Park):

  • Day hiking on Table Mountain, Lion's Head, and Pipe Track is free — no permit needed
  • Hoerikwaggo Trail overnight permit required — book via SANParks reservations
  • Kirstenbosch Botanical Garden entry fee applies for Skeleton Gorge start

CapeNature reserves:

  • Day permits required for most CapeNature trails — pay at the gate or book online
  • Jonkershoek: cutoff time 18:00, permits at the reserve gate
  • Cederberg (Algeria): minimum 3 adult hikers, permit from camp office
  • Cederberg (Sanddrif): permits from Sanddrif Private Nature Reserve
  • Robberg: conservation fee at entrance gate. Check tides before the Point Circuit
  • Kogelberg: Three Sisters Trail is free; all other trails require a permit
  • Pack it in, pack it out — take all your rubbish home
  • Stay on the marked trail to protect fragile fynbos — it is one of the most biodiverse ecosystems on Earth
  • Do not pick flowers or disturb plants
  • Do not light fires except in designated braai areas
  • Bury human waste at least 70m from water sources and trails
  • Leave historical and cultural artefacts in place
  • Keep noise levels down — you're in a wild space
